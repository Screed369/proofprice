Consumer prices aggregated microdata: August 2026
This worksheet includes microdata outputs published monthly alongside the Office for National Statistics' consumer price inflation monthly statistical release. The microdata presented here are used in the calculation of the Consumer Prices Index including owner occupiers' housing costs (CPIH), Consumer Prices Index (CPI) and the Retail Prices Index (RPI). 

The microdata in this worksheet refer to the monthly period: August 2026.

Please refer to the January 2026 microdata article (URL link below) for information relating to the calculation of all microdata outputs included in this worksheet.

This worksheet has been edited to meet accessibility guidelines. 
Publication dates
The data tables in this worksheet were originally published at 07:00 on 16 September 2026. Crown copyright © 2026.
Notes
Some cells in the tables refer to notes, which can be found in the Notes worksheet. Note markers are presented in square brackets, for example: [note 1].

Values of [x] in these tables represent data that are not available.

Values of [z] in these tables represent data that are not applicable.

Notes

Note number |Note text 
note 1|The CPIH and CPI measures of consumer price inflation are designated as Accredited Official Statistics (previously known as National Statistics). However, the RPI and its derivatives do not meet the required standards for designation as Accredited Official Statistics. A full report on the removal of the RPI’s accreditation is available on the UK Statistics Authority website (see the "UK Statistics Authority" website link on the "Cover Sheet" worksheet).
note 2|The data presented in this worksheet are not part of the Accredited Official Statistics suite of consumer price inflation statistics. They are published to support user research and understanding of consumer price inflation data.
note 3|Additional CPIH, CPI, and RPI data are available on the ONS website. Website links to related ONS consumer price inflation datasets are provided on the "Cover Sheet" worksheet.
note 4|As sample sizes at the consumption segment and regional levels are smaller than those at higher aggregation levels, these lower-level indices may be more volatile. Users are advised to consider longer-term trends rather than short-term movements, and to interpret these outputs within the wider context of the supplementary information provided (see the "Consumer price inflation statistical bulletins" website link on the Cover Sheet worksheet).
note 5|Definitions of each column in the tables presented in this worksheet are available in our "Changes to the provision of microdata outputs for consumer price inflation statistics: January 2026" article (linked on the Cover Sheet worksheet).

In worksheets 2a and 2b, the definitons of the indicator markers can be found in Section 6.2.6, "Indicator codes", of the "Consumer Prices Indices Technical Manual 2019" (linked on the "Cover Sheet" worksheet).
note 6|From March 2026, where applicable, COICOP Divisions 1 and 2 (groceries) have been calculated by aggregating groceries scanner data with data collected via existing methods, often referred to as "local collection". All other COICOP Divisions continue to use local collection methods only.
note 7|Groceries scanner data refers to data collected by retailers at the point of sale (in-store or online). Scanner data consist of sales records for all transactions within each period, including total expenditure and quantity sold. Groceries scanner data were incorporated into our consumer price inflation measures from the 25 March 2026 release. For the part of the grocery market that is not currently covered by retailer scanner data, we continue to collect prices manually through existing methods (in-store and online), known as "local collection".
note 8|This table includes data from both local collection and grocery retailer scanner data. Further details on how each of the microdata outputs within this worksheet have been produced are available in our microdata article (linked on the "Cover Sheet" worksheet).
note 9|This table provides regional-level breakdowns of consumer price indices for consumption segments that have regional stratification. Consumption segments without regional stratification are not included.
note10|For Divisions 1 and 2, where groceries scanner data are incorporated, data for all consumption segments are published in this worksheet. For all other divisions, data are published only for consumption segments with non-confidential status.
note 11|A CPIH_REGION_INDEX column has not been included in this table because its values would be the same as those in the CPI_REGION_INDEX column.
note 12|The weights in this worksheet sum to 1 for each consumption segment, when summed across all regions for a given consumption segment.
note 13|This table provides counts and proportions of local collection indicator markers for all items across the basket. These indicator markers, assigned by price collectors, flag special features of price quotes such as sales, temporary stock issues, or product changes.
note 14|This table is produced using only local collection data. Grocery retailer scanner data are not included in the microdata in this table. Further details are available in our "Changes to the provision of microdata outputs for consumer price inflation statistics: January 2026" article (linked on the "Cover Sheet" worksheet).
note 15|The indicator marker analysis data consist of two output tables, both using local collection data only. The "all quotes" table contains all price quote data. The "valid quotes" table contains the subset of price quotes used to produce the published inflation statistics, including only those considered valid following the quality assurance process. Valid quotes exclude those flagged as erroneous.

The dataset is structured across three hierarchy levels (ID_TYPEs): Item, COICOP5, and Grand Total. Item-level is the most granular, providing metadata for individual items (for example, "Basmati Rice"). COICOP5 groups quotes for items mapped to a given COICOP5-level category (for example, "Rice"). Grand Total sums all quotes across the basket.
note 16|Some proportional metrics in worksheet "2b" of this dataset have been omitted, as the values for all entries in these columns would be 0.
note 17|Certain items are mapped at the COICOP5-level to "RPI ONLY ITEM" because they are not included in the CPI(H) basket and therefore do not form part of the COICOP structure used for this output. These items have been retained to preserve full coverage of all products present in the price quotes dataset.
note 18|Values of [x] in these tables represent data that are not available.

For example, where a row contains fewer than 10 observations, the sample size is considered too small for reliable results. In such cases, a row for this record will still be provided, but all numerical metrics will be marked with "[x]" to indicate that the data are "not available".
note 19|Values of [z] in these tables represent data that are not applicable.

For example, for a row that represents COICOP5 counts, entries in the "CS_ID" and "CS_DESC" columns will be marked with "[z]" to indicate that these cells are "not applicable".
note 20|All estimates in this worksheet have been calculated using unrounded data and, where appropriate, outputs have been rounded for presentation in this worksheet. Users should be aware that due to rounding differences, using the rounded estimates presented here in onward calculations may produce slightly different results from those produced using unrounded data.

For example, if users use the rounded region weights to aggregate the rounded regional-level consumption segment indices to national level, they could estimate the consumption segment indices at national level. Due to rounding, there may be small differences between these results (calculated using rounded data) and the national-level consumption segment indices which we publish separately in our monthly "Consumer price inflation consumption segment indices and price quotes datasets" website link on the "Cover Sheet" worksheet, which are calculated using unrounded data and therefore are more accurate.
