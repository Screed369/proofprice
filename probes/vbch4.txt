<p>The terms of use for datasets of the federal authorities on opendata.swiss are determined by the existing legal framework. If there is a statutory requirement to cite the source, this is indicated by the appropriate symbol next to the dataset concerned. In all other cases, users are recommended to cite the source.</p>
<p>The symbols next to each dataset indicate what use is permitted. All data owners use the same symbols.</p>

<h2 id="modele" aria-level="2"><span data-contrast="none">Usage model</span></h2>
<p>opendata.swiss provides a usage model for other Swiss authorities and organisations with a mandate from the state. Please <a href="https://opendata.swiss/en/contact">contact</a> us if you have any questions.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="apropos" aria-level="2"><span data-contrast="none">Licences versus terms of use</span></h2>
<p><strong>Creative commons (CC) licences</strong> are legal contracts that allow copyright holders to<strong> establish terms for the reuse</strong> of their work, while retaining their rights. By contrast, <strong>terms of use</strong> can include technical or organisational rules, even where no copyright protection exists. In Switzerland, <strong>raw data or datasets</strong> (e.g. temperature recordings, demographic statistics) are <strong>not generally protected</strong> by copyright (source: <a href="https://www.unil.ch/unil/en/home/menuinst/recherche/open-science/open-research-data/conformite-exigences/licences.html">User licences – UNIL</a>). That is why the datasets on opendata.swiss are generally accompanied by terms of use, rather than licences. This approach seeks to <strong>maximise transparency</strong> for users, while respecting the legal framework applicable to each type of content published.</p>
