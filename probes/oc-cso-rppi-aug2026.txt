<div class="release-key-findings">
    <h3 class="pub-findings-header">Key Findings</h3>

    <ul class="pub-findings-list">

        <li><p>The national Residential Property Price Index (RPPI) increased by 5.1% in the 12 months to August 2026, down from the 5.4% recorded in the 12 months to July 2026.</p></li>

        <li><p>Property prices in Dublin rose by 3.9% and prices outside Dublin were up by 6.2% compared with August 2025.</p></li>

        <li><p>As of August 2026, the Residential Property Price Index outside of Dublin is more than three times higher than in May 2013, when it hit its lowest point since the series began in 2005.</p></li>

        <li><p>The median price of a dwelling purchased in the 12 months to August 2026 was &euro;400,000.</p></li>

        <li><p>The highest median price for a dwelling in the 12 months to August 2026 was &euro;685,000 in D&uacute;n Laoghaire-Rathdown, while the lowest median price was &euro;199,000 in Longford.</p></li>

        <li><p>In August 2026, 4,279 dwelling purchases by households were filed with the Revenue Commissioners at a total value of &euro;1.95 billion. These purchases were made up of 3,241 existing dwellings and 1,038 new dwellings.</p></li>

        <li><p>Revenue data shows there were 1,811 first-time buyer purchases filed in August 2026.</p></li>

        

    </ul>
</div>

    <div class="pub-comment">
        <h3>Statistician's Comment</h3>
        <p>The Central Statistics Office (CSO) has today (13 October 2026) published the Residential Property Price Index (RPPI) August 2026.</p>
<p>Commenting on the release, Samantha Walsh, Statistician in the Prices Division, said: &ldquo;<em>Residential property prices rose by 5.1% in the 12 months to August 2026, down from the 5.4% recorded in the 12 months to July 2026. In Dublin, residential property prices rose by 3.9%, while residential property prices outside Dublin were 6.2% higher in August 2026 when compared with August 2025.</em></p>
<p><em>The annual increase in residential property prices nationally of 5.1% for August 2026 represents the lowest annual rise since December 2023, when the annual increase was 4.1%.</em></p>
<p><em>As of August 2026, the Residential Property Price Index outside of Dublin is more than three times higher than in May 2013. In May 2013, in the midst of the economic downturn, the RPPI for properties outside of Dublin hit its lowest point since the series began in 2005.</em></p>
<h4>Residential Property Price Index</h4>
<p><em>In the 12 months to August 2026, house prices in Dublin rose by 3.3% while apartment prices increased by 5.7%. </em><em>The highest house price growth in Dublin was in </em><em>D&uacute;n Laoghaire-Rathdown</em><em> at 6.0% while </em><em>Dublin City </em><em>saw a rise of 1.0%.</em></p>
<p><em>Outside of Dublin, house prices were up by 6.0% and apartment prices rose by 8.6%. The region outside of Dublin that saw the largest growth in house prices was the Border (Cavan, Donegal, Leitrim, Monaghan, and Sligo) at 9.1%, while at the other end of the scale, the Mid-East </em><em>(Kildare, Louth, Meath, and Wicklow) and the South-West (Cork, and Kerry) both saw a rise of 4.9%.</em></p>
<h4>Median Property Prices</h4>
<p><em>Households paid a median or mid-point price of &euro;400,000 for a residential property in the 12 months to August 2026.&nbsp;The highest median price paid for a dwelling was &euro;685,000 in D&uacute;n Laoghaire-Rathdown, while the lowest was &euro;199,000 in Longford.</em></p>
<p><em>The most expensive Eircode area over the 12 months to August 2026 was A41 (Ballyboughal, Dublin) with a median price of &euro;865,000, while F45 (Castlerea, Roscommon) had the least expensive median price of &euro;160,000.</em></p>
<p><em>A&nbsp;</em><a href="https://data.cso.ie/table/HPM08"><em>table of median prices by Eircode area</em></a><em>&nbsp;is available, as is our&nbsp;</em><a href="https://visual.cso.ie/?body=entity/rppi"><em>Interactive App</em></a><em>&nbsp;where you can explore the median property price by Eircode area.</em></p>
<h4>Volume and Value of Purchases</h4>
<p><em>In August 2026, 4,279 dwelling purchases by households at market prices were filed with the Revenue Commissioners, an increase of 2.5% when compared with the 4,174 purchases in August 2025. The total value of transactions filed in August 2026 was &euro;1.95 billion. This was made up of 3,241 existing dwellings with a value of &euro;1.44 billion, and 1,038 new dwellings with a value of &euro;506.4 million.&rdquo;</em></p>

<h3 class="pub-findings-header ">Residential Property Price Index - Overview</h3>
<p>Residential property prices rose by 5.1% in the 12 months to August 2026, down from 5.4% in the 12 months to July 2026. In Dublin, residential property prices saw an increase of 3.9%, down from 4.5% in the 12 months to July 2026. Property prices outside Dublin were 6.2% higher in August 2026 when compared with a year earlier, an increase from the 6.1% in the 12 months to July 2026 (<em>See Figure 1 and Table 1</em>).</p>

<h3 class="pub-findings-header ">Residential Property Price Index by Region</h3>
<p>In the 12 months to August 2026, house prices&nbsp;nationally rose by 4.9% while apartment prices increased by 6.6%. In Dublin,&nbsp;house prices grew by 3.3%,&nbsp;and apartment prices&nbsp;were up by 5.7%.&nbsp;Outside Dublin, house prices were up by 6.0%&nbsp;while apartment prices rose by 8.6%.</p>
<p>In Dublin, the highest house price growth was in D&uacute;n Laoghaire-Rathdown at 6.0% while Dublin City&nbsp;saw a rise of 1.0%. The regions outside of Dublin that saw the largest growth in house prices were the Border (Cavan, Donegal, Leitrim, Monaghan, and Sligo) at 9.1%, and the Midlands (Laois, Longford, Offaly, and Westmeath) at 8.8%. At the other end of the scale, the Mid-East (Kildare, Louth, Meath, and Wicklow) and the South-West (Cork, and Kerry) saw a 4.9% rise in house prices (<em>See Figure 2</em>).</p>
