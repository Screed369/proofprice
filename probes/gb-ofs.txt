    <h3>Notes for providers considering increasing fees</h3>
<p>We wrote to accountable officers on 10 July 2026 to confirm that we will not be collecting information about fees for 2027-28 through the annual fee information process this year, so there is nothing you need to submit to the OfS for approval in relation to fees for 2027-28. We’ll set out our expectations for the collection of fee information for future years when required.</p>
<p>We expect you to take steps to provide relevant fee information to your students following the publication of further information about changes to fee limits for 2026-27, including:</p>
<ul>
<li>If you’re considering raising fees within prescribed limits for new entrants, or for continuing students in line with inflation, then we strongly advise you to consider your compliance with <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/higher-education-consumer-law-advice-for-providers">Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) guidance</a> and consumer protection law, including in circumstances where your contracts with students provide for changes to fees.</li>
<li>If you are making changes that increase fees for new entrants in line with prescribed limits, you should make sure that prospective students have access to information about the full cost of their course, for the duration of the course, before they commit themselves to undertaking a higher education course.</li>
<li>In making any changes that would impact on fee levels for continuing students, you should note that continuing students cannot be charged more than the lower of either: the relevant prescribed fee limit or the level to which fees can be increased in line with the inflationary statement recorded in the access and participation plan that was in effect in the year of the student’s entry to their course.</li>
</ul>
<h3 class="MsoNormal">Changes to fee limits due to LLE</h3>
<p class="MsoBodyText">The lifelong learning entitlement (LLE) introduces credit‑based tuition fee limits. This will work across all higher-level courses and modules funded by the LLE, regardless of whether students study them on a full-time, part-time or accelerated basis. The fee limit will directly relate to the amount of credits in the course, rather than the number of academic years that are studied.</p>
<p class="MsoBodyText">We expect you to take steps to ensure that you and your governing body are aware of the fee limits that will apply for 2027-28 and to provide relevant fee information to current and prospective students.</p>
