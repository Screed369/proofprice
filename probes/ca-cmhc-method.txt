                <h2>Survey definitions  and methodology </h2>
<p>CMHC conducts the annual Rental Market Survey in October to estimate the  relative strengths in the rental market. The survey is conducted on a sample  basis in all urban areas with populations of 10,000 or more. It targets only  privately initiated structures with at least 3 rental units, which have been on  the market for at least 3 months. Social and affordable housing structures or units are not included in the Rental Market Survey frame.</p>
<p>The survey collects market rent levels, availability<sup>1</sup>, turnover and  vacancy unit data for all sampled structures. Data is collected using a  combination of telephone interviews and site visits. Information is obtained  from the: </p>
<ul>
    <li>owner </li>
    <li>manager, or </li>
    <li>building superintendent </li>
</ul>
<p>The survey is conducted during the first 2 weeks of October and the  results reflect market conditions at that time. </p>
<p>CMHC&rsquo;s Rental Market Survey provides a snapshot of vacancy and turnover  rates and average rents in both new and existing structures. The survey also  provides a number of measures for average rent and change in rent, as follows: </p>
<p><strong>Average rent: </strong>This represents rent  levels in both new and existing structures. This is a weighted average of all  units combined, whether vacant or occupied.</p>
<p><strong>Average rent of  occupied units: </strong>This represents rent levels in both new and existing structures for  units which the respondent <strong class="underline">has not</strong> identified as vacant (see below  definition of vacant). </p>
<p><strong>Average rent of  vacant units: </strong>This represents rent levels in both new and existing structures for units  which the respondent <strong class="underline">has identified</strong> as vacant. This is also referred to  as &ldquo;asking&rdquo; rent, that is, the rent the owner or property manager is asking for  the unit. </p>
<p><strong>Average rent of  turnover units: </strong>This represents rent levels in both new and existing structures where  the respondent has identified units which have turned over in the past 12  months (see below definition of turnover). </p>
<p><strong>Average rent of  non-turnover units: </strong>This represents rent levels in both new and existing structures for all  remaining units which have not turned over in the past 12 months.</p>
<p><strong>Percentage change  (%) in average rent: </strong>This provides an estimate of rent level movement by  eliminating the compositional effects of new structures, conversions and survey  sample rotation. The estimate is based on existing rental structures that were  common to the survey sample for both the previous year and the current Rental  Market Survey year. It is important to note that some compositional effects can  remain due to instances such as when an existing rental unit is  renovated/upgraded or there is turnover of the rental unit. The estimated  percentage change in average rent is only published if this estimate is  statistically different than zero (0) as determined by a statistical test of  significance. Rent levels in new and existing structures are also published;  however, changes in rents that may be calculated based on new and existing  structures may not have the same significance. </p>
<h2>Definitions</h2>
<p><strong>Universe:</strong> This consists of  all row projects and apartment structures with three or more units. The  universe is presented as a sum of all rental units. </p>
<p><strong>Rental Apartment  Structure:</strong> Any building containing 3 or more rental units, of which at least 1  unit is not ground oriented. Owner-occupied units are not included in the  rental building unit count. </p>
<p><strong>Rental row  (townhouse) structure: </strong>Any building containing 3 or more rental units, all  of which are ground oriented, side-by-side, with common walls dividing each rental unit. Owner-occupied  units are  not included in the  rental building unit count. These row units in some centres are commonly  referred to as townhouses. </p>
<p><strong>Vacancy: </strong>A unit is considered  vacant if, at the time of the survey, the unit is physically unoccupied and  available for immediate rental Available for immediate rental means a new lease  has not been signed or the unit is not undergoing major renovations. </p>
<p><strong>Turnover: </strong>A unit is  counted as being turned over if it was occupied by a new tenant who moved in  during the past 12 months. A unit can be counted as being turned over more than  once in a 12-month period.</p>
<p><strong>Availability: </strong>A rental unit is  considered available if:</p>
<ul>
    <li>the existing tenant has given, or has received, notice to move </li>
    <li>a new tenant has not signed a lease </li>
    <li>the unit is vacant (see above definition of vacant) </li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Rent:</strong> The rent refers to  the actual amount tenants pay for their unit. No adjustments are made for the  inclusion or exclusion of amenities and services such as heat, hydro, parking  or hot water (i.e., utilities such as heating, electricity and hot water may or  may not be included in the rent. For available and vacant units, the rent is  the amount the owner is asking for the unit. The average rents reported in this  publication provide a sound indication of the amounts paid by unit size and  geographical sector. </p>
<p><strong>Rental arrears: </strong>A unit is in arrears  if the tenant is late paying rent by one month or more. The total dollar amount  is a weighted estimate of the total dollar value amount in rent in arrears as  of the end of September for the given Rental Market Survey year. Similarly, the  number of units in arrears is a weighted estimate of the total units which are  late paying rent. This data is reported by CMHC at the CMA level &mdash; with the  exception of Charlottetown CA &mdash; and for all centres 10,000+ combined. Data is  aggregated for all bedroom types combined. </p>
<h2>Data reliability  measures</h2>
<p>CMHC does not publish a statistic if its reliability is too low or if  publication of a statistic would violate confidentiality rules. </p>
<p>Unit counts (universe) are released as these are not estimates. However,  for confidentiality, all other estimates &mdash; such as vacancy rates and average  rent &mdash; are released only if a given estimate is based on 4 or more responding  entities. </p>
<p>For reliability, the ability to publish an estimate is determined by the  coefficient of variation (CV) for that estimate. A letter code representing the  statistical reliability (for example, the CV) for each estimate is provided to  indicate the data reliability. The CV of an estimate is defined as the ratio of  the standard error of the estimate to the estimate itself and the CV is  generally expressed a percentage. For example, let the average rent for  one-bedroom apartments in each CMA be x̄ and its standard error be &sigma;x̄. Then  the Coefficient of Variation is given by CV = &sigma;x̄/x̄. </p>
<h2>Reliability codes  for proportions</h2>

<ul>
    <li>A &mdash; Excellent </li>
    <li>B &mdash; Very good </li>
    <li>C &mdash; Good </li>
    <li>D &mdash; Poor (use with caution) </li>
    <li>** &mdash; Data suppressed </li>
</ul>

<h2>Reliability codes  for averages and totals </h2>
<p>CMHC uses the coefficient of variation to determine the reliability  level of the estimates of average rents and a coefficient of variation cut-off  of 10% for publication of totals and averages. It is felt that this level of  reliability best balances the need for high quality data and not publishing  unreliable data. </p>
<p>CMHC assigns a level of reliability as follows ( the coefficient of  variations are given in percentages): </p>
<ul>
    <li>A &mdash; If the CV is greater than 0 and less than or equal to 2.5 then the  level of reliability is Excellent. </li>
    <li>B &mdash; If the CV is greater than 2.5 and less than or equal to 5 then the  level of reliability is Very Good. </li>
    <li>C &mdash; If the CV is greater than 5 and less than or equal to 7.5 then the  level of reliability is Good. </li>
    <li>D &mdash; If the CV is greater than 7.5 and less than or equal to 10 then the  level of reliability is Poor. </li>
    <li>** &mdash; If the CV is greater than 10 then the estimate is suppressed and  not published. </li>
</ul>
<h2>Other symbols </h2>
<p>Other denotations used in presenting Rental Market Survey data: </p>
<ul>
    <li>++ refers to a change in rent is not statistically significant. This  means that the change in rent is not statistically different than zero (0).  This applies only to &ldquo;Estimate of Percent Change of Average Rent&rdquo; tables. </li>
    <li>&mdash; means that no units exist in the universe for this category </li>
    <li>n/a means not applicable/not available </li>
</ul>
<p>Get all the information by  reading the <a href="/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-data/data-tables/rental-market">Rental Market Survey &mdash; Data Tables</a>. </p>
<p><strong>Note</strong>: The April version  of this survey was conducted for the last time in 2015. The availability rate  was collected between 2005 and 2017 exclusively; this series was discontinued  in 2018.</p>
<p class="note"> <sup>1 </sup>The availability estimate was calculated between 2005 and 2017; this estimate has since been discontinued.</p>
